# RP2040 vendor notes

## GPIO overview
The RP2040 has 30 user GPIOs (GP0..GP29). The Raspberry Pi Pico board exposes GP0..GP22 and GP26..GP28; GP23..GP25 and GP29 are used on-board (GP25 drives the user LED). Every GPIO selects one function through IO_BANK0 GPIOx_CTRL.FUNCSEL (1=SPI, 2=UART, 3=I2C, 4=PWM, 5=SIO, 6=PIO0, 7=PIO1). Pad settings (pull-up, pull-down, drive strength, input enable) live in PADS_BANK0. GP0/GP1 default to UART0 and are reserved for the debug console in Silo Labs designs.

## I2C pin mux
I2C0 SDA: GP0, GP4, GP8, GP12, GP16, GP20. I2C0 SCL: GP1, GP5, GP9, GP13, GP17, GP21.
I2C1 SDA: GP2, GP6, GP10, GP14, GP18, GP26. I2C1 SCL: GP3, GP7, GP11, GP15, GP19, GP27.
The canonical Pico pairing is I2C0 on GP4 (SDA) / GP5 (SCL). Enable internal pull-ups with gpio_pull_up() when the breakout has none.

## SPI pin mux
SPI0 RX(MISO): GP0, GP4, GP16. SPI0 CSn: GP1, GP5, GP17. SPI0 SCK: GP2, GP6, GP18. SPI0 TX(MOSI): GP3, GP7, GP19.
SPI1 RX: GP8, GP12, GP28. SPI1 CSn: GP9, GP13. SPI1 SCK: GP10, GP14, GP26. SPI1 TX: GP11, GP15, GP27.
Drive CSn as a plain SIO GPIO when talking to devices that need CS held across multiple transfers.

## UART pin mux
UART0 TX: GP0, GP12, GP16, GP28. UART0 RX: GP1, GP13, GP17.
UART1 TX: GP4, GP8, GP20. UART1 RX: GP5, GP9, GP21.
uart_init(uart1, 115200) returns the actual baud rate achieved by the fractional divider (UARTIBRD/UARTFBRD).

## ADC and PWM
ADC inputs are GP26 (ADC0), GP27 (ADC1), GP28 (ADC2), GP29 (ADC3, VSYS/3 on the Pico); ADC4 is the internal temperature sensor. The ADC is 12-bit, 500 ksps, clocked from CLK_ADC at 48 MHz.
PWM: 8 slices with two channels each. Slice = (gpio >> 1) & 7; even GPIOs are channel A, odd GPIOs are channel B. Frequency = clk_sys / (DIV * (TOP + 1)). A 50 Hz servo signal at 125 MHz uses DIV=64 and TOP=39061.

## Clock tree
XOSC is a 12 MHz crystal. PLL_SYS: REFDIV=1, FBDIV=125 gives VCO 1500 MHz, POSTDIV1=6, POSTDIV2=2 gives 125 MHz for CLK_SYS. PLL_USB produces 48 MHz for CLK_USB and CLK_ADC. CLK_PERI (UART/SPI) is normally fed from CLK_SYS. CLK_REF runs from XOSC at 12 MHz. ROSC is the ~6.5 MHz boot oscillator. pico-sdk's runtime init configures all of this before main().

## pico-sdk idioms
Include pico/stdlib.h and hardware/<peripheral>.h. Call stdio_init_all() first; with pico_enable_stdio_usb(target 1) printf goes to USB CDC. I2C: i2c_init(i2c0, 400*1000); gpio_set_function(4, GPIO_FUNC_I2C); gpio_set_function(5, GPIO_FUNC_I2C); i2c_write_blocking / i2c_read_blocking with a 7-bit address. SPI: spi_init(spi0, 1000*1000) and spi_write_read_blocking. Timing: sleep_ms, sleep_us, time_us_64(). Bit-banged single-wire sensors (DHT22, DS18B20) need gpio_set_dir switching and microsecond timeouts.

## Reset and bring-up
Peripherals are held in reset at boot; RESETS.RESET bits must be cleared and RESETS.RESET_DONE polled before touching registers. pico-sdk init functions (i2c_init, spi_init, uart_init, adc_init) perform this reset cycle. The DW_apb_i2c block must be disabled (IC_ENABLE.ENABLE=0) before writing IC_CON, IC_TAR, or the SCL count registers.

## Known pitfalls
USB enumeration timing errata are handled inside pico-sdk's TinyUSB port; prefer pico_enable_stdio_usb over hand-rolled USB. GP23..GP25 and GP29 are not on the Pico header. I2C requires pull-ups on SDA/SCL; without them the bus reads stuck low.
